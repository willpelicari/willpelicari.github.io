Cataloc — Privacy Policy

Last updated: 25 September 2026

The short version

Cataloc does not collect anything. There is no account, no server, no analytics, no advertising and no third-party SDK that reports on you. Everything you put into the app stays on your device unless you deliberately send it somewhere.

There are four ways data can leave your phone, and you have to set up every one of them: pairing with another phone on your Wi-Fi, syncing through a shared folder whose address you supply, sharing a list as a web page, and exporting your data. In none of them does anything pass through the developer, who has no access to your catalogue by any route.

There is a fifth way data can leave Cataloc without leaving your phone, and it is off until you switch it on: letting your phone's own assistant and search find your things. It is described in full under Your phone's assistant and search.

What Cataloc stores, and where

Cataloc stores the following in its own private storage on your device:

  • The households, containers and items you create, including their names, tags, quantities, best-before dates and loan details.
  • The photos you take or choose, saved as files inside the app's own folder.
  • A history of changes you have made within the app.
  • Your settings: language, appearance, which household you last opened, whether you have allowed your phone's assistant and search to see your things, whether you have allowed an assistant to propose adding one, whether the one-time "enjoying it?" question has already been asked, the name this device goes by in a household's history, and what the store last told the app about your plan — whether PRO is active, and whether it was bought outright. That last pair is a copy of the store's own answer, kept so that opening the app without a network does not lock a paying customer out of their own catalogue. It is not a record of a payment, and it holds nothing about how you paid.
  • If you have set up a shared folder, the password for it, and the key derived from your folder passphrase if you have locked one. Both are kept in the device's own secure storage — the Keychain on iOS, the Keystore on Android — rather than alongside the catalogue.

Households are kept separate from one another: each has its own catalogue, search and history, and nothing passes between them.

None of this is transmitted anywhere by default. It is not backed up to any service run by the developer, because no such service exists.

Your device's own backup (iCloud Backup on iOS, or Google's backup on Android) may include this data if you have those features enabled. That is between you and Apple or Google, and is governed by their privacy policies.

Syncing a household across devices

Syncing is entirely opt-in. Neither method is set up out of the box, and there is no Cataloc account or Cataloc server in either one.

Pairing two phones directly

  • One phone shares a household and displays a QR code and a single-use code, valid for five minutes. The other phone must present that code to connect.
  • The two phones then exchange that household directly over your local network: its containers, items, tags, history, member names, and the photos attached to them.
  • Nothing is sent to the internet by this method. There is no server in the middle, no relay, and no copy retained anywhere by the developer.
  • The connection is open only while the invite or join screen is on screen. Cataloc does not listen on your network in the background and does not advertise itself.

Syncing through a shared folder

Instead of pairing in person, you can point several devices at one shared folder and let them keep each other current. This is off until you configure it under Me → Sync.

  • You choose the folder. It might be a drive on your own network or hosting space of your own. The sync screen names a few providers that hand you a WebDAV address, purely as examples of where to look — Cataloc has no default provider, no arrangement with any of them and no folder of its own, and the developer never learns the address you use.
  • It runs on its own once you have set it up. Cataloc checks the folder when you open the app, and publishes what you changed about ten seconds after you stop changing it. There is no button and no schedule: the app reaches the address you gave it while you are using it, and at no other time. It never syncs in the background.
  • A copy of the household — items, photos and history — is written to that folder. Anyone who can read the folder can read your catalogue, so treat access to it exactly as you would access to the data itself.
  • Protection in transit is whatever the address you entered provides: an https:// folder is encrypted in transit, an http:// one is not. Prefer https://.
  • You can lock the folder. Under Me → Sync, "Lock this folder" encrypts everything Cataloc writes there — the containers, items, names, dates and the photos — on your phone, before it is sent. Whoever keeps the folder then holds files they cannot read. You choose a passphrase and type the same one into every phone that syncs there; it never leaves your devices, and there is no copy of it anywhere else, which also means nobody can recover it for you. Anything written to the folder before you turned the lock on stays there unencrypted until you delete it yourself, from the folder. Even with the lock on, whoever keeps the folder can still see how many devices publish to it, how many photos there are and how large each file is — never a name, a date or a picture.
  • Whoever operates that folder — a hosting company, or you — is responsible for it, under their terms rather than this policy.

Either way, each device keeps its own complete copy. Removing a member on one device does not delete the copy already on the other — it only stops future syncs. Once you sync a household with someone, they hold a full copy of it, exactly as if you had handed them an export file.

Device name. Each install has a name — taken from your device's own name unless you change it — and a random identifier. These are shared only with devices and folders you have set up, so that changes can be attributed to a person in the household's history. They are never sent anywhere else.

Sharing, labels and export

Sharing a list. You can turn a shelf or a tag into a single web page, photos included, and hand it to your device's share sheet. It opens in any browser without an app or an account — which also means anyone who receives the page or its link can see everything on it, so share it only with people you would show the shelf to. If you switch on the "ask to borrow" button, you type in a WhatsApp number or an email address, and it is written into the page itself so that tapping the button opens a message to you. It travels with the file: anyone the page is forwarded to has that number or address, not only the person you sent it to. Left off — which is how it starts — nothing of the sort is included.

Labels. A printed label can carry a QR code, a short code and the container's name. Scanning the QR opens that container in Cataloc. Each of those elements can be switched off before printing, so a box that leaves the house need not carry its name or location on the outside. Printing or sharing a label produces a PDF through your device's own print and share systems.

Export everything. This writes a single JSON file containing your data and photos, and hands it to your device's share sheet. Where that file then goes is entirely your choice. Cataloc does not upload it anywhere.

Your phone's assistant and search

This is off when you install Cataloc. You are asked about it plainly while setting up, and you can change the answer at any time under Me → Assistant. It is free, and consent is the only thing that turns it on: there is no subscription anywhere in this, and nothing at all happens until you say yes.

What it does

When you turn it on, Cataloc writes a list of your things into your phone's own search index — the one behind Spotlight on iPhone and behind system search on Android. For each container and item that list holds its name, the full path to where it is kept, its household's name, its tags, its short code, a reference to its photograph, how many of it there are, its best-before date if it has one, and — if it is out on loan — the name you typed for whoever has it.

That is what lets you search your phone rather than the app, and what lets you ask Siri or your Android assistant where something is and be told which shelf it is on.

The borrower's name is the one piece of this that is not yours. Ask where the drill is while it is lent out and the answer is "lent to Marta", because that is the useful answer — but it also means a name you typed about somebody else sits in your phone's index for as long as the loan is open. Marking the thing returned takes it straight back out, as does turning this setting off. If you would rather no borrower's name ever reached the index, either do not record who has your things or leave this setting off.

What this does and does not mean

  • Nothing is uploaded. The index is on your phone. There is still no Cataloc server, and the developer still has no way to see any of it.
  • It does leave Cataloc. This is the honest qualification to "everything stays in the app's own storage" above. The index belongs to the operating system, not to Cataloc, and what can read it is governed by Apple's and Google's rules rather than by this policy. On iPhone, Apple's own privacy policy covers Siri, Spotlight and Apple Intelligence; on Android, Google's covers system search and the assistant.
  • Every household is included while the setting is on. There is no per-household switch.
  • Turning it off removes it. Switching the setting off, or using "Remove everything from search", takes the whole list back out of the system index. Deleting a container or item removes it from the index too, as does letting your subscription lapse.

Letting the assistant add things

There is a second, separate setting, off unless you turn it on, which lets your assistant offer to add something to your catalogue.

An assistant can never write to your catalogue. When you ask one to add something, Cataloc opens with what it heard already filled in, and nothing is saved until you confirm it. Assistants mishear and they invent; this setting changes what they can propose, never what they can do.

Photographing a whole shelf

Cataloc PRO lets you take one photograph of a shelf and drag a box around each thing on it, giving each one a name. Every thing you mark is saved with its own crop of that picture.

Nothing looks at the photograph but you. Cataloc does not recognise, classify or otherwise analyse what is in a picture — it has no such feature — and no artificial intelligence of any kind is involved in adding things to your catalogue. The boxes are where you drew them and the names are what you typed.

The cropping happens on your phone. The photograph is not uploaded, and the original picture of the shelf is discarded once the things you marked have been saved.

Permissions

Camera — requested when you tap "Take photo", and when you scan a label's QR code or a pairing code. Photographs are written to the app's storage and are not uploaded. Declining the camera does not block anything: you can still pick pictures from your library, and items do not require a photo at all.

Photo library — requested only when you tap "Library". Cataloc reads only the single picture you select and stores a copy in its own folder.

Local network (iOS) — requested the first time you sync, so that two phones can find each other on your Wi-Fi, or so Cataloc can reach a shared folder held on your own network. Used only for syncing.

Cataloc requests no other permissions. It does not use location, contacts or the microphone. It sends no notifications and shows no badges, so it never asks to notify you.

What Android lists on the store page. Android prints every permission an app declares, including the ones granted automatically and never prompted for. Besides the camera, Cataloc's declares three: network access, used for syncing and nothing else; vibration, which React Native declares by default and Cataloc never actually uses; and reading and writing shared storage on Android 12 and older, which is the old way of doing what the photo picker does on newer versions. That is the whole list. There is no location, no contacts, no microphone, and nothing that lets the app draw over other apps.

In-app purchases

Cataloc is free for one household and 200 containers and items. Cataloc PRO lifts both limits and adds one of the features described above — photographing a whole shelf and marking several things on it at once. Letting your phone's assistant and search see your things is free. PRO is $2.99 per month, $14.99 per year, or $29.99 as a single payment that never renews.

If you subscribe, the transaction is handled entirely by Apple or Google, using the payment method already on your Apple or Google account. Cataloc never sees your payment details, and there is no Cataloc account: renewals, price changes, refunds and cancellation are all handled by the store, and cancelling is done there rather than here. The developer receives the payout and the store's standard sales reporting, which contains no personal data.

Cataloc records only a local flag noting whether the store currently reports an active subscription, so the app works correctly when it is offline.

Nothing you have already catalogued is ever removed, hidden or made read-only, whether you subscribe, never subscribe, or subscribe and then stop.

Deleting your data

"Delete all data" on the Me tab erases every household, container, item, photo and history entry from the device permanently, and takes everything back out of your phone's search index along with it. Deleting the app does the same.

Making it smaller. Me → Storage is a gentler version of the same thing: it re-encodes your photographs at a lower resolution and can drop history older than a date you choose. Both are permanent, both show you the exact number of megabytes and lines they will remove before you confirm, and neither sends anything anywhere.

Because there is no server, there is no copy for the developer to hold, and therefore nothing to request the deletion of. Export first if you want to keep a copy — once it is gone, it is gone.

Copies you have already sent elsewhere are outside the app's reach: a shared sync folder, a device you paired with, an exported file or a shared page all have to be deleted where they now live.

Children

Cataloc is not directed at children and collects no personal information from anyone, including children.
